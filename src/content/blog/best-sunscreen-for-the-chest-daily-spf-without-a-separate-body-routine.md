## What to read on the label

"Invisible" and "for face" on the front of a bottle do not tell you whether the formula will sit under a cotton neckline.

Useful clues:

1. **Broad spectrum.** UVA and UVB both matter on weekday chest skin, not only on beach days.
2. **A clear SPF number.** SPF 30 or 50 is the usual shopping range. Higher is not a reason to apply less.
3. **Texture.** Fluid, lotion, dry-touch, or a stick for edges. Pick one you will finish on collarbones.
4. **Dry-down.** A fast-set formula is easier under a shirt than a shiny cream that transfers.
5. **Fragrance.** Scent does not equal protection. It can make a chest test harder to live with under fabric.
6. **Water resistance.** Useful if you sweat. Not a reason to skip a later pass if the shirt comes off.

Do not treat "body" on the label as proof the product will cover the chest well. Coverage is about how much you spread, not the word on the bottle.

## Mistakes that leave the chest exposed

1. **Calling leftover face SPF "chest coverage."** The film on your hands is not a layer.
2. **Saving body SPF for vacation.** Weekday collars still show skin.
3. **Dressing while the chest is shiny.** The shirt lifts product and leaves patchy spots.
4. **Using less sunscreen so it will not stain.** A smear that stays clean can still be a weak layer.
5. **Adding a brightening chest serum the same week.** You will not know whether the SPF, the serum, or the shirt caused the sting.
6. **Stopping at the neck and hoping gravity helps.** Sunscreen does not travel down a shirt.

The smaller change is often enough: the weekday bottle you already tolerate, one extra pass, and a minute before the tee.
