## What to skip while testing a new SPF

The fastest way to misjudge sunscreen is to test it during a full routine overhaul.

Skip starting a new SPF beside:

- A new exfoliating toner
- A new retinol or stronger night active
- A new rich cream under sunscreen
- A new gripping primer
- Rubbing sunscreen until it pills, then adding more on top
- Judging shade match under bathroom bulbs only
- Skipping moisturizer on flaky areas because the sunscreen says hydrating
- Using a tiny amount because a full layer looks heavy

If the sunscreen fails, you want to know whether the problem was finish, prep, amount, tint, or pilling. Too many changes make every SPF look guilty.

## FAQ

### What sunscreen finish is best for textured skin?

Many textured-skin routines do best with a lightweight fluid, gel, serum, hybrid, or clear primer-like finish. The best finish depends on whether your texture is dry, oily, bumpy, flaky, or prone to pilling.

### Does tinted sunscreen help textured skin?

Tinted sunscreen can soften the look of uneven tone, redness, or old marks, but it will not flatten texture. Shade match matters. A poor tint can make texture look more obvious.

### Why does sunscreen pill on textured skin?

Pilling often comes from layer conflict, too much product underneath, rubbing too long, or applying sunscreen before moisturizer settles. Try fewer layers, lighter prep, and two thin SPF passes.

### Should I use moisturizer before sunscreen if my skin is textured?

Use moisturizer where skin feels dry or flaky. If your T-zone gets slick, use less there. Sunscreen usually looks smoother when dry patches are comfortable first.

### Is mineral or chemical sunscreen better for textured skin?

Neither is automatically better. Mineral sunscreen can feel reassuring but may look chalky or dry on texture. Chemical and hybrid sunscreens can feel thinner but may sting some eyes. Compare finish and comfort, not just filter type.

### Can sunscreen make pores look bigger?

Some formulas can make pores look more obvious if they are shiny, thick, chalky, or pilling. A thinner fluid, clear SPF, or soft natural finish may sit better.
