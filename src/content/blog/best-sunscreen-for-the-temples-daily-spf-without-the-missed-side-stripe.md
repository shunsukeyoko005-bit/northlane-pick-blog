# Best Sunscreen for the Temples: Daily SPF Without the Missed Side Stripe

I have left the house with a finished-looking center of my face, glasses on for the school run, and later found a tender stripe where the frames sat. The cheeks looked fine. The temples did not.

The trap is treating the temples like leftover forehead. Bathroom light hits the front of your face first. Hair covers the sides. Glasses, a clip, or a hat brim hide the same strip until later, when sidelight or a photo shows a two-tone line.

If nothing changes, another season becomes the same loop: a sore band beside the eyes, extra concealer that does not solve the gap, and the feeling that sunscreen worked everywhere except the sides of your face.

This guide names why the temples need their own pass and how to cover them without adding a separate makeup or hair project.

## The short answer

The best sunscreen for the temples is a broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher that you can apply generously from the outer brow to the hairline, set without sliding into glasses or hair, and reapply after frames, sweat, or a hat move the layer. For many people, that means a comfortable face sunscreen first, then a small second pass on both temples before you put glasses on.

## Why the temples get skipped

The temples sit at the edge of the face. They do not get the same frontal bathroom light as cheeks. Hair covers them until a clip, ponytail, or wind moves that cover. Glasses rest on the same strip all day. Hats, phone calls, and rubbing your forehead also thin the layer.

The fix is not a ten-step side-of-face routine. It is a better sequence: apply your main SPF, add a small second pass from the outer brow to the hairline on both sides, then put glasses on after the layer has had a moment to set.

## What to look for in sunscreen for the temples

Start with a sunscreen you can use on the face, then treat the temples as a high-wear edge.

Useful clues:

1. **Broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher.** A temple-focused plan still starts with a real daily sunscreen layer.
2. **A format you can place on a small strip.** A stick is easier than hoping leftover forehead lotion migrated sideways.
3. **Texture that spreads without tugging at hair.** Rubbing into the hairline often leaves a missed strip and a greasy part.
4. **Finish that does not fog or smear glasses.** Greasy formulas can migrate onto lenses and frames.
5. **Low-scent or fragrance-free language if scent bothers you.** Temple skin sits close to hair products, perfume, and glasses pads.
6. **A touch-up format you will carry.** A stick in the bag beats a perfect bottle left at home.

The goal is not a separate product for every body part. It is to stop pretending one rushed center-face swipe covers the strip that glasses, hair, and sidelight keep finding.

## A simple morning routine for the temples

Keep the routine short enough to repeat.

Morning:

1. Cleanse gently or rinse if your skin does not need a full wash.
2. Apply a light moisturizer only where the temples feel tight.
3. Apply sunscreen to the face, including the forehead and cheeks.
4. Add a small second pass from the outer brow to the hairline on both temples.
5. Wait before putting glasses on if your schedule allows.
6. Reapply later with a stick if frames, sweat, or a hat wiped the layer.

## What to skip while testing a new temple-friendly SPF

Make the sunscreen test boring on purpose.

For the first week or two, avoid starting it beside:

- A new exfoliating acid
- A new retinoid or retinal
- A strong vitamin C serum
- A fragranced cleanser
- A new hair oil or spray on the same skin
- Aggressive scrubbing to remove sunscreen
- Several new "calming" products in the same cart

This is not about making skincare precious. It is about knowing whether the sunscreen was comfortable, or whether the whole routine was too loud.
