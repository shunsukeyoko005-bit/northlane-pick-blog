## What to read on the label

"For face and body" on a listing is marketing. The label is the test.

Useful clues:

1. **Broad spectrum.** UVA and UVB both matter on a rim that sits in daylight and window light.
2. **A clear SPF number.** SPF 30 or 50 is the usual shopping range. Higher is not a reason to apply less.
3. **Format.** Stick, fluid, lotion, or mineral cream. Pick the one you can land on a small, curved surface.
4. **Water resistance.** Useful if you sweat under a hat. Not a reason to skip a later pass.
5. **Fragrance.** Scent near the ear canal can be harder to live with than the same scent on cheeks.
6. **Cast.** White leftover is more obvious on the rim than on the forehead.

Do not treat "natural," "for babies," or "sensitive" on the front of the package as proof it will sit well on a tender ear. This article is not advice about children's skin.

## Mistakes that make ear SPF less useful

1. **Stopping at the jaw.** The rims still burn.
2. **Using a hat as sunscreen.** Shade helps. Gaps around the brim still take light.
3. **Putting glasses and earrings on while SPF is wet.** Product transfers, then those spots get less coverage.
4. **One decorative swipe.** If you cannot see or feel product on the back of the ear, you probably missed it.
5. **Calling a stick the whole morning face layer.** Use it for edges after a real face SPF, or apply enough stick to actually cover.
6. **Testing a new earring metal, a new fragrance, and a new SPF together.** You will not know which step stung.

The smaller fix is often better: face SPF you already tolerate, then one extra stick pass on the ears.
