## What to read on the label

"Invisible" and "weightless" on the front of a bottle describe finish, not whether the formula will stay put over your cream.

Useful clues:

1. **Broad spectrum.** UVA and UVB both matter on a weekday face.
2. **A clear SPF number.** SPF 30 or 50 is the usual shopping range. Higher is not a reason to apply less.
3. **Texture.** Fluid, gel, dry-touch lotion, or a primer-like cream. Pick one that sets before the next step.
4. **Finish words.** Dry-touch, ultra-light, and unseen-style formulas can sit with fewer crumbs if you give them a minute.
5. **Fragrance.** Scent does not cause pilling, but it can make a test harder to live with.
6. **Water resistance.** Useful if you sweat. Not a reason to skip reapplication.

Do not treat "makeup-ready" as proof the product will ignore a wet moisturizer. Those words are marketing. The dry-down is the test.

## Mistakes that make pilling worse

1. **Rubbing the next product in while SPF is still shiny.** Wet layers grab and roll. Set layers stay put more often.
2. **Wiping the crumbs and calling the face done.** That is thinner coverage, not a texture fix.
3. **Stacking serum, cream, primer, and SPF in the same test week.** You will not know which step rolled.
4. **Using less sunscreen so it "sits better."** A smear that does not pill can still be a weak layer.
5. **Scrubbing foundation over a fresh SPF.** Press or stipple. A long rub lifts product.
6. **Testing a new self-tanner or peeling acid on the same mornings.** Flake plus SPF is an easy crumb day.

The smaller change is often enough: one fast-dry formula you already tolerate, fewer layers underneath, and a minute before the next step.
