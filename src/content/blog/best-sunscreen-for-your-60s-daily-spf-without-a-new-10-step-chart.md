## FAQ

### Do you need a special sunscreen just because you are in your 60s?

Not a special identity product. You need a broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher you will actually apply. Texture and comfort often change in this decade. The protection job does not.

### Can I keep using the sunscreen I liked in my 40s?

Maybe, if the texture still feels comfortable and the current label matches how you use it. If the leftover bottle makes you apply less, your skin is telling you the finish no longer fits. For expiration basics, see [sunscreen expiration](https://northlanepick.com/blog/sunscreen-expiration-when-to-replace-your-spf-bottle/).

### Should I moisturize before SPF in my 60s?

Often yes on dry zones. A thin cream can make sunscreen spread with less tugging. If the cream is too thick, wait a minute or use less so SPF does not pill or settle.

### Is mineral sunscreen better after 60?

Not automatically. Some zinc formulas feel comforting. Others dry down tight or look chalky in lines. Compare finish and how much you will actually apply.

### Do indoor days still need sunscreen?

If you sit near windows, run errands, or walk to the car, daily SPF is still the more useful habit. For that pattern, see [best sunscreen for indoor days](https://northlanepick.com/blog/best-sunscreen-for-indoor-days-daily-spf-without-waiting-for-sunshine/). Saving the bottle for vacation is how the 60s skip starts.

### Should sunscreen in your 60s also include retinol or acids?

I would not make that the first test. Get a comfortable daily SPF working, then decide whether extra actives belong in the same week. More jobs on one bottle can mean more reasons to skip it.

### How long should I test one sunscreen?

If it stings or makes skin feel worse, stop. If it feels comfortable, keep the rest of the morning routine steady for several days before deciding whether it earns the sink space.
