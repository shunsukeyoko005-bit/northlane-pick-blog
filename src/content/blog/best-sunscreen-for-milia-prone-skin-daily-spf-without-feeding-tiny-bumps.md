## What to check before buying

You do not need a dermatology chart before coffee. A few label clues make the first pass easier.

Helpful checks include:

1. **SPF 30 or higher and broad spectrum wording.** Tiny bumps are not a reason to skip coverage or use less product.
2. **Fluid, gel, milk, lotion, cream, or stick texture.** Start lighter. Cream and balm SPF can be fine for some faces, but they are a harder first test when leftover film is the complaint.
3. **Non-comedogenic or oil-free wording if you want a filter.** Not a guarantee. Still a useful search filter when cream SPF feels like frosting.

## FAQ

### Does sunscreen cause milia?

Not as a simple rule. Some people notice tiny bumps when a formula is heavy, stacked with occlusives, or rubbed into the same spots. That is a reason to compare lighter textures and simpler layering. It is not a reason to skip SPF. This article does not diagnose milia or treat it.

### Should I use less sunscreen if I am bump-prone?

No. Use enough for coverage, then change texture if the finish feels like frosting. A pea-sized smear of thick SPF is a worse weekday habit than a full layer of a lighter fluid.

### Is mineral sunscreen better for milia-prone skin?

Not automatically. Some mineral creams feel thicker and invite rubbing. A dry-touch mineral can be a better comparison than a rich zinc cream. Wearability matters more than the filter type on the label.

### Can I wear this under makeup?

Many people do. Let the SPF set first. If makeup pills, simplify the layers underneath and test the sunscreen alone for a few days.

### What if the bumps are around my eyes?

Keep sunscreen out of the eyes, use a light layer, and avoid rubbing. If the area stings, swells, or will not calm down, stop testing and ask a licensed professional.
