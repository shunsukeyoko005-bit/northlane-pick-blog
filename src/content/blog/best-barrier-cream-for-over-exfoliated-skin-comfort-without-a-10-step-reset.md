## What to read on the label

"Barrier" on the front of a jar can mean many things. The ingredient list and the directions matter more than the marketing name.

Useful clues:

1. **Fragrance.** A fragrance-free option is easier to test when skin already feels reactive.
2. **Texture.** Cream, balm-cream, lotion, or ointment. Pick what you will actually repeat.
3. **Occlusive ingredients.** Petrolatum, dimethicone, and similar ingredients help reduce water loss from the skin surface.
4. **Humectants.** Glycerin, hyaluronic acid, and panthenol can help skin feel hydrated, especially under cream.
5. **Barrier-support extras.** Ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids, colloidal oatmeal, and allantoin can fit this lane.
6. **No exfoliating add-ons.** A "repair" cream with acids may not be the calmest test after overdoing exfoliation.

Do not treat a dramatic claim as proof the cream fits your face. A plain product you will use for a week is more useful than a complicated one you quit after two nights.

## Mistakes that keep the cycle going

1. **Buying a barrier cream and keeping the acid toner twice a day.** The routine is still loud.
2. **Switching cleanser, cream, and sunscreen together.** You will not know which product changed the feel.
3. **Using a rich cream under too little SPF.** If the layer feels heavy, choose a lighter morning texture instead of skimping on sunscreen.
4. **Expecting one night to undo weeks of overdoing it.** Give the simple routine time to be readable.
5. **Choosing fragrance because the jar feels more special.** A plain test is easier when skin already feels prickly.
6. **Treating tightness as a reason to exfoliate more.** Dry flakes do not automatically mean your face needs another peel.
