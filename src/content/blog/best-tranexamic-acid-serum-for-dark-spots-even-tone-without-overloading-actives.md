## What to read on the label

Product names change. "Brightening" does not tell you what is in the bottle.

Useful clues:

1. **Tranexamic acid in the ingredient list.** If you cannot find it, keep comparing.
2. **Extra acids.** Tranexamic plus glycolic, retinol, and vitamin C in one bottle is a noisier test.
3. **Fragrance.** Scent can make a tone test harder to read.
4. **Texture.** Watery serum, gel, or cream-serum. Choose what you will use before moisturizer.
5. **Directions.** Look for simple use, not a 10-step order.
6. **SPF reminder.** A tone serum without daytime protection is a half plan.

Do not treat "clean" or "natural" as proof it will fade the look of spots.

## Mistakes that make a tone serum less useful

1. **Skipping SPF because you bought a fade serum.** The spots you care about still see daylight.
2. **Adding glycolic, retinol, and vitamin C in the same week.** You will not know what helped or stung.
3. **Expecting a weekend change.** Tone products are slower than a school-week mood.
4. **Using it on a stripped, tight face.** Fix cleanser and moisturizer first.
5. **Piling it under too many layers before makeup.** Pilling can look like "the serum failed."
6. **Judging in one harsh bathroom bulb.** Check daylight.

The smaller fix is often better: one tranexamic serum, moisturizer, and a sunscreen you will repeat.
