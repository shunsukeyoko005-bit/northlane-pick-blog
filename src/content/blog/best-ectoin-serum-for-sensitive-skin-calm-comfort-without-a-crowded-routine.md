## What to look for on the label

Start with boring label clues. Sensitive skin routines usually fail from too many variables, not from a lack of interesting ingredients.

Helpful clues include:

1. **Ectoin in a simple serum format.** A serum, ampoule, or lightweight gel is easier to place than a mask or peel-style product.
2. **Fragrance-free if scent bothers you.** Fragrance can make a calming test harder to read.
3. **Humectants and comfort ingredients.** Glycerin, hyaluronic acid, panthenol, beta-glucan, allantoin, and ceramides can fit well beside ectoin when the formula stays simple.
4. **No crowded active story.** If the same product also promises strong exfoliation, retinoid-like smoothing, and brightening, it may not be the quiet test you wanted.
5. **Clear routine directions.** The listing should tell you whether it goes before cream, morning, night, or both.
6. **Texture comments.** Watery, gel, milky, fast-absorbing, and layers under SPF are useful review clues.

Be careful with "calming" products that rely on a strong scent, a long active list, or a sticky finish you know you will avoid by Wednesday.

## A simple testing plan

Use one ectoin serum for several ordinary days while the rest of your routine stays familiar.

Track:

- Does it sting, flush, or feel warm?
- Does it feel sticky under moisturizer or sunscreen?
- Does skin feel less tight after cleansing?

- Are you willing to use it on a rushed weekday?
