# Best Sunscreen for Melasma-Prone Skin: Daily SPF Without a Brightening Overload

## What to read on the label

"Melasma" on a listing is marketing. The label is the test.

Useful clues:

1. **Broad spectrum.** UVA and UVB both matter for daily wear.
2. **A clear SPF number.** SPF 30 or 50 is the usual shopping range. Higher is not a reason to apply less.
3. **Finish you will blend.** Fluid, hybrid, tinted, or mineral. Pick the one that does not make you skip.
4. **Fragrance.** Scent can make a reactive-feeling face harder to read.
5. **Extra actives.** SPF plus acids, high-dose vitamin C, and retinoids in one bottle is a noisier test.
6. **Tint.** A sheer tint can hide cast. It is not a full concealer, and it still needs enough product.

Do not treat "clean" or "natural" as proof it will sit well on patches you already watch.

## Mistakes that make SPF less useful here

1. **Dabbing a little on patches only.** The rest of the face still sees daylight.
2. **Stacking three brightening products, then rushing SPF.** The layer that matters gets skipped.
3. **Judging cast only in bathroom light.** Check a window or the car.
4. **Skipping reapplication because morning felt "medical."** Pickup sun still counts.
5. **Using face lotion as SPF.** Unless the lotion is labeled sunscreen and you apply enough, it is not the plan.
6. **Pausing sunscreen when a serum stings.** Pause the serum. Keep SPF if your skin can stand a simple formula.

The smaller fix is often better: one sunscreen you will finish, moisturizer only where you are dry, and a stick for later.
