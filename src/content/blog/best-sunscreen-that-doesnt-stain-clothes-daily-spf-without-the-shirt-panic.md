## What to read on the label

"Invisible" on the front of the bottle is marketing. The label and the dry-down are the test.

Useful clues:

1. **Broad spectrum.** UVA and UVB both matter on a chest that sits in window light.
2. **A clear SPF number.** SPF 30 or 50 is the usual shopping range. Higher is not a reason to apply less.
3. **Texture.** Fluid, dry-touch lotion, gel, stick, or mineral cream. Pick one that sets before you dress.
4. **Tint.** Untinted is easier on white shirts. Tinted can mark fabric if you pull clothes on too soon.
5. **Fragrance.** Scent can cling to cotton longer than the protection conversation.
6. **Water resistance.** Useful if you sweat under a collar. Not a reason to skip reapplication.

Do not treat "sheer," "weightless," or "no white cast" as proof the product will stay off a white tee. Those words describe skin finish, not laundry.

## Mistakes that make shirt stains worse

1. **Dressing while SPF is still shiny.** Wet product transfers. Set product is less likely to.
2. **Wiping the neckline clean to save a shirt.** That is skipped coverage, not a stain strategy.
3. **Using tinted SPF under a white collar too soon.** Pigment can mark fabric the same way leftover foundation does.
4. **Spraying sunscreen onto a body already dressed.** Product on cotton is not coverage on skin, and it can spot the shirt.
5. **Calling a stick the whole morning layer.** Use it for edges after a real face and chest SPF, or apply enough stick to actually cover.
6. **Testing a new self-tanner and a new SPF together.** You will not know which step stained.

The smaller fix is often better: one fast-dry formula you already tolerate, then a minute of waiting before the shirt.
