## What to look for on the label

The front of the bottle can help. The Drug Facts panel and whether you will apply a real layer are the test.

Useful clues:

1. **Broad spectrum.** Face, neck, chest, and the backs of the hands still sit in daylight and window light.
2. **A clear SPF number.** SPF 30 or 50 is the usual shopping range. Higher is not a reason to apply less.
3. **Texture language.** Fluid, lotion, cream, mineral, unseen-feel, or hydrating. Pick the one that matches today's dryness or shine.
4. **Fragrance-aware shopping.** Scent is harder to live with if your face now feels warmer or more reactive.
5. **Short directions.** A formula that needs several primers and a long set time may not survive a weekday.
6. **Makeup notes if you still wear a little coverage.** Look for comments about pilling, sliding, and wait time.

Do not treat "postpartum," "hormone support," or "recovery" on a marketing line as proof the formula will sit well. Those words are shopping clues at best. The weekday test is whether you will leave a real layer on.

## What to skip while testing

The best test is boring.

For the first week or two, avoid starting a new sunscreen beside:

- A new exfoliating acid
- A new retinoid
- A new vitamin C serum if your skin already feels touchy
- A new cleanser, moisturizer, and SPF at the same time
- Fragrance-heavy products that make your face warm
- Three sunscreen textures bought because each one sounded possible

This is not about a perfect lab routine. It is about giving the bottle a fair chance on a real morning.

## A simple testing plan

Use one sunscreen for several ordinary days while the rest of your routine stays familiar.

Track:

- Does it sting, flush, or feel tight after five minutes?
- Does it look shiny, chalky, or settled in texture by lunch?
- Did you apply enough, or did you use a thin swipe because the morning ran out?
- Did you add any new serum, cream, or fragrance the same week?
- Are you willing to use it on a rushed weekday?

My note is usually one line: bottle, morning, tight or shiny, applied enough or not. If the note needs a paragraph, I changed too many things.

## Quick shopping checklist

Before buying, ask:

- Is it broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher?
- Can I apply a real amount in two minutes?
- Does the texture match the skin I have now, not the skin I had last year?
- Is it fragrance-free if scent bothers me?
- Will it sit over a thin moisturizer without pilling?
- Can I keep cleanser and night products familiar while I test?
- Do I have a stick or extra pass for neck, ears, and hands?
- Have I asked a clinician about new actives if I am nursing or still recovering?

If those answers point to one bottle, start there. If the product needs a new serum, new cream, and new makeup to make sense, keep comparing.
