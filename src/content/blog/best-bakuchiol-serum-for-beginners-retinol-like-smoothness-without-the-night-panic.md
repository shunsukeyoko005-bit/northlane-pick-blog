## What to read on the label

Product names change. Percent claims change. "Retinol alternative" marketing is not a substitute for the ingredient list.

Useful clues:

1. **Bakuchiol in the name or INCI list.** If you cannot find it, keep shopping.
2. **Fragrance.** Scent can make a "gentle" night serum harder to tolerate.
3. **Extra actives.** Bakuchiol plus retinoid plus AHA plus vitamin C in one bottle is a harder test.
4. **Texture.** Serum, oil, or cream. Choose what you will actually put on after a short shower.
5. **Directions.** Night use, frequency, and whether sunscreen is mentioned for morning.
6. **Patch test language.** A small jawline test still beats a full-face first night.

Do not treat "natural" or "clean" as proof it will feel easy on your skin.

## Mistakes that make bakuchiol feel pointless

1. **Stacking it with retinol the first week.** You will not know which product caused the tightness.
2. **Using it every night from day one.** A few nights is enough information.
3. **Adding a new acid the same week.** Keep exfoliants familiar or paused.
4. **Skipping moisturizer.** Many night serums feel better with a cream over them.
5. **Skipping morning SPF.** A smoother-looking routine still needs daytime protection.
6. **Judging after one dramatic night.** Texture changes are usually slower than a viral before-and-after.
7. **Buying the busiest "anti-aging" blend.** Extra ingredients make a beginner test noisier.

The fix is usually smaller: one bakuchiol product, a cleanser you already like, a cream you already like, and sunscreen in the morning.

## A simple testing plan

1. Choose one bakuchiol serum, cream, or oil.
2. Read the current label and patch test.
3. Use it at night two times the first week if your skin is cautious.
4. Keep cleanser, moisturizer, and morning sunscreen the same.
5. Do not use retinol or a new exfoliant on those same nights.
6. Watch for stinging, warmth, dryness, flaking, bumps, or next-day tightness.
7. Continue only if your skin feels steady.

I keep the notes plain: date, product, night used, and "fine" or "not fine." That tiny record beats trying to reconstruct the week while packing lunches.
