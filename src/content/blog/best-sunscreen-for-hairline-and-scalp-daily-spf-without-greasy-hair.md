## What to read on the label

"Scalp" on a listing is marketing. The label is the test.

Useful clues:

1. **Broad spectrum.** UVA and UVB both matter on a part that sits in daylight.
2. **A clear SPF number.** SPF 30 or 50 is the usual shopping range. Higher is not a reason to apply less.
3. **Format.** Stick, powder, spray, fluid, or lotion. Pick the one you can land on skin, not hair.
4. **Water resistance.** Useful if you sweat under a hat. Not a reason to skip reapplication.
5. **Fragrance.** Scent near the hairline can be harder to live with than the same scent on cheeks.
6. **Cast.** White or gray leftover is more obvious on a dark part than on a forehead.

Do not treat "natural," "for babies," or "sensitive" on the front of the package as proof it will sit well on a tender scalp. This article is not advice about children's skin.

## Mistakes that make hairline SPF less useful

1. **Stopping at the forehead.** The first half-inch of hairline still burns.
2. **Using a hat as sunscreen.** Shade helps. Gaps around the part still take light.
3. **Dragging lotion through the roots.** Product on hair is not coverage on skin.
4. **One decorative powder pass.** If you cannot see product on the part, you probably did not reach skin.
5. **Calling a stick the whole morning face layer.** Use it for edges after a real face SPF, or apply enough stick to actually cover.
6. **Testing a new scalp serum and a new SPF together.** You will not know which step stung.

The smaller fix is often better: face SPF you already tolerate, then one extra format for the part and hairline.

## A simple testing plan

1. Choose one hairline or scalp format: stick, powder, or targeted spray.
2. Read the current label for SPF, broad spectrum, and fragrance.
3. Patch test on intact skin near the temple if you are cautious.
4. Use it as your only new SPF helper for several weekday mornings.
5. Keep night products familiar. Do not add a new acid or retinoid in the same window.
6. Watch for sting, tightness, flakes, greasy roots, or a cast you will not wear.
7. Continue only if you will actually put it on exposed skin, not on hair.
