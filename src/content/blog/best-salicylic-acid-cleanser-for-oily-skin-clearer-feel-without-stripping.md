## What to read on the label

"Deep clean" on the front of the bottle does not tell you whether the formula fits oily skin.

Useful clues:

1. **Salicylic acid or BHA.** Look for clear wording on the label or ingredient list.
2. **Cleanser type.** Gel, cream-gel, foaming, or medicated-style cleanser all feel different.
3. **Fragrance.** Scent can make a test harder to read if your skin is reactive.
4. **Other actives.** Benzoyl peroxide, acids, retinoids, or strong brightening claims in the same routine can make irritation more likely.
5. **Use directions.** A cleanser that encourages frequent use may not be the right first test for cautious skin.
6. **Texture after rinsing.** Clean is good. Tight, hot, or squeaky is a warning sign.

Do not judge the cleanser by foam alone. A big lather can feel satisfying and still be too much for your face.

## Mistakes that make salicylic acid cleanser harder to use

1. **Washing more often because skin feels oily.** More washing can create more tightness without solving the shine pattern.
2. **Pairing it with an exfoliating toner right away.** Keep one active variable at a time.
3. **Scrubbing longer for better results.** Follow the label and rinse well.
4. **Skipping moisturizer.** Oily skin can still feel dehydrated or tight.
5. **Blaming sunscreen for every greasy feel.** Sometimes the cleanser is too harsh; sometimes the SPF texture is the mismatch.
6. **Using it on the whole face when only the T-zone needs it.** Combination skin may need different frequency by zone.

The smaller fix is usually easier to repeat: one cleanser plan, one moisturizer that does not feel heavy, and sunscreen you can actually wear.
