## What to read on the label

"For daily use" on the front of a bottle does not tell you whether you will wear it on a laptop morning.

Useful clues:

1. **Broad spectrum.** Indoor window light is more of a UVA story than a sunburn story. UVA still counts through a lot of ordinary glass.
2. **A clear SPF number.** SPF 30 or 50 is the usual shopping range. Higher is not a reason to apply less because you are staying home.
3. **Texture words.** Fluid, gel, gel-cream, dry-touch, ultra-light, or clear-feel. Those are easier to keep on a weekday than a thick sport cream.
4. **Water resistance.** Helpful if pickup turns sweaty. Not required as the main indoor-day job.
5. **Fragrance.** A strong scent can make a desk-day test harder to live with.
6. **Cast.** A leftover white film makes people apply less on days they "do not need it."

Skip the idea that indoor days deserve a weaker product. A formula you will finish on weekdays is more useful than a high-drama bottle you only open in July.

## Mistakes that make indoor-day SPF easy to drop

1. **Applying too little because you are staying home.** Indoor is not a half-dose setting.
2. **Waiting for obvious sunshine.** Daylight through glass does not wait for a beach bag.
3. **Stacking a heavy cream underneath.** The mix can sit on the surface and make you blot.
4. **Touching your face while it is still wet.** A laptop morning turns a setting layer into a smear.
5. **Leaving the stick at home.** Indoor days still grow errands.
6. **Keeping a sport lotion as your only face SPF.** A texture you dislike on weekdays will stay in the cabinet.

A lighter texture is useful. A missing layer is not.
