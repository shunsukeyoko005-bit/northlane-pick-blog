## What to read on the label

"Eczema" on a listing is marketing. The label is the test.

Useful clues:

1. **Broad spectrum.** UVA and UVB both matter for daily wear.
2. **A clear SPF number.** SPF 30 or 50 is the usual shopping range. Higher is not a reason to apply less.
3. **Fragrance.** Scent can make a reactive-feeling face harder to read.
4. **Mineral filters.** Zinc oxide is a common first comparison. Titanium dioxide may appear too. Check the current Drug Facts panel.
5. **Extra actives.** SPF plus acids, retinoids, or strong brightening claims is a noisier test.
6. **Texture you will blend.** Cream, lotion, fluid, or hybrid. Pick the one that does not make you skip.

Do not treat "natural," "for babies," or "sensitive" on the front of the package as proof it will sit well on a patch you already watch. This article is not advice about children's skin.

## Mistakes that make SPF less useful here

1. **Skipping sunscreen because cream already feels thick.** Labeled SPF is still the daylight step.
2. **Testing a new cleanser, serum, and SPF together.** You will not know which step stung.
3. **Rubbing a dry patch to "blend."** Gentle spreading beats friction.
4. **Judging sting in the first 30 seconds under bathroom light.** Give it a few weekday mornings if the formula is simple and your skin is intact.
5. **Using body eczema cream on the face as SPF.** Unless it is labeled sunscreen and you apply enough, it is not the plan.
6. **Pausing sunscreen when a serum stings.** Pause the serum. Keep a simple SPF if your skin can stand it.

The smaller fix is often better: gentle cleanse, moisturizer only where you are dry, and one sunscreen you will finish.

## A simple testing plan

1. Choose one daily sunscreen.
2. Read the current label for SPF, broad spectrum, and fragrance.
3. Patch test on intact skin if you are cautious.
4. Use it as your only new SPF for several weekday mornings.
5. Keep night creams familiar. Do not add a new acid, retinoid, or vitamin C in the same window.
6. Watch for sting, tightness, warmth, bumps, or a cast you will not wear.
7. Continue only if you will actually apply enough.
