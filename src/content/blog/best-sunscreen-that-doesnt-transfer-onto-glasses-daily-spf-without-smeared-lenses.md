## What to read on the label

"Invisible" on the front of the bottle does not tell you whether it will sit on a lens.

Useful clues:

1. **Texture words.** Fluid, gel, gel-cream, dry-touch, ultra-light, or clear-feel. Those tend to set faster than a thick cream SPF.
2. **Broad spectrum and a clear SPF number.** SPF 30 or 50 is the usual shopping range. A lighter feel is not a reason to apply a thinner coat under frames.
3. **Dry-down language.** Matte, dry-touch, or "sets quickly" matters more here than water resistance for a weekday commute.
4. **Fragrance.** Scent near the eyes can make you keep adjusting frames, which restarts the smear.
5. **Cast.** A leftover white film on the nose bridge is easy to wipe onto lenses. Blend it, then wait.
6. **Stick format for edges.** A stick is a later pass for the nose and temples, not a replacement for the morning layer.

Skip the idea that glasses wearers need a weaker product. A formula that dries, plus a short wait, is more useful than a sport lotion you wipe off to see the road.
