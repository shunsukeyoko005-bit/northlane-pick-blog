## What to look for on the label

Start with the current label and product page, because formulas and listings change.

Useful clues include:

1. **Broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher.** Redness-prone cheeks still need real daily sunscreen coverage.
2. **Fragrance-aware language.** Fragrance-free or low-scent formulas can make testing easier if scent is a known trigger for you.
3. **Mineral filter options.** Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide formulas can be worth comparing if chemical filters often feel stingy on your cheeks.
4. **Texture that spreads without tugging.** A thick paste can make you rub too much, even if the ingredient list looks calm.
5. **Hydrating support.** Glycerin, panthenol-style comfort ingredients, and a flexible finish may help if cheeks feel tight before SPF.
6. **No need for a dramatic glow claim.** Redness-prone skin is easier to read when the sunscreen is not trying to be primer, treatment, fragrance, and shimmer at once.

The goal is not a perfect label. It is a sunscreen you can use enough of without negotiating with your cheeks every morning.

## A simple morning routine for redness-prone cheeks

Keep the routine short enough to repeat.

Morning:

1. Cleanse gently or rinse if your skin does not need a full wash.
2. Apply a light moisturizer only where cheeks feel tight.
3. Apply sunscreen in sections instead of rubbing one big amount across the face.
4. Press and spread over cheeks, then stop once coverage is even.
5. Wait before makeup if your schedule allows.
6. Reapply later with a gentle touch-up method, especially after sweating or wiping your face.

The part I watch is pressure. If I am late and rubbing like I am cleaning a counter, my cheeks usually tell me by lunch.

- A new exfoliating acid
