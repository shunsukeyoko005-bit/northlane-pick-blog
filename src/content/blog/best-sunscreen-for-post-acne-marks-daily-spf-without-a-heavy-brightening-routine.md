## What to look for in sunscreen for post-acne marks

Start with the sunscreen you will use consistently, then compare finish and layering around your current routine.

Useful clues include:

1. **Broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher.** The mark-focused routine still starts with a real daily sunscreen layer.
2. **Texture that lets you apply enough.** If you hate the feel, you will probably use a thin amount.
3. **Low-pilling finish over serums.** Brightening routines often include niacinamide, vitamin C, azelaic acid searches, or moisturizer, so sunscreen has to sit over real layers.
4. **Comfort when skin is between breakouts.** A formula that stings or feels greasy can make you skip the step exactly when you need consistency.
5. **Reapplication plan.** A morning layer helps, but long outdoor days, sweating, and rubbing need a touch-up plan.

Do not make sunscreen responsible for every mark. If you are dealing with deep texture changes, painful acne, or marks that do not behave like your usual post-breakout pattern, a professional opinion may be the simpler path.

## How to layer sunscreen with common post-acne mark products

Keep the routine short enough to repeat on a school morning, work morning, or any morning where the sink has to be shared.

Morning:

1. Cleanse gently or rinse if your skin does not need a full wash.
2. Apply one familiar serum or treatment if your skin already tolerates it.
3. Moisturize dry areas before sunscreen.
4. Apply broad spectrum sunscreen generously.
5. Let it settle before makeup if your schedule gives you the minute.
6. Reapply on long outdoor days, after sweating, or after wiping your face.

Night:

1. Remove sunscreen and makeup without scrubbing.
2. Cleanse gently.
3. Use your usual acne or brightening step if it already fits your routine.
4. Moisturize.
5. Avoid starting several new actives in the same week.

The quiet part matters. If I start a new cleanser, serum, retinoid, sunscreen, and foundation in the same week, every mark becomes a mystery instead of a routine note.
