## How to layer polyglutamic acid in a short routine

Keep the order simple:

1. Cleanse gently.
2. Pat skin so it is slightly damp or comfortable, depending on the product directions.
3. Apply a thin layer of polyglutamic acid serum.
4. Wait about a minute.
5. Apply moisturizer.
6. Apply sunscreen in the morning.
7. Use makeup only after the layers settle.

If the serum pills, use less, wait longer, or move it to night. A product that fights sunscreen every morning is not practical, even if the ingredient list looks perfect.

At night, PGA can sit between cleanse and cream. I like that lane better for formulas that feel a little tacky, because nobody is waiting on me to blend foundation while lunch boxes are open.

## FAQ

### Is polyglutamic acid better than hyaluronic acid?

Not automatically. They are different hydration lanes. Hyaluronic acid may already be enough for some routines, while polyglutamic acid is worth comparing if you want a different texture or if your current hydrating serum feels gone too quickly.

### Can I use polyglutamic acid every day?

Follow the current product label. Many hydrating serums are designed for regular use, but start with a small amount and keep the rest of the routine steady while you test.

### Does polyglutamic acid replace moisturizer?

No. Treat it as a serum layer, not the seal. If skin feels tight by morning, moisturizer may be the missing step.

### Can oily skin use PGA?

Yes, if the texture fits. Oily-dehydrated skin often does better with a thin serum and a lighter moisturizer than with mattifying every step until the face feels tight underneath.

### Why does my hydrating serum pill under sunscreen?

You may be using too much, layering too quickly, or combining textures that do not sit well together. Try less serum, wait longer, or move the serum to night if mornings are rushed.
