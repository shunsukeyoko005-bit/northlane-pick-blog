## What to check before buying

You do not need an acid glossary before bedtime. A few listing clues tell you whether a lactic serum fits a dry-skin routine you can repeat.

Helpful checks include:

1. **Percent.** 5% is a calmer first test than 10% or a "peel" number. Higher can wait.
2. **Base texture.** Milky, hydrating, and cream-serum formulas often feel kinder on flakes than a thin, stingy liquid.
3. **Fragrance level.** Fragrance-free is easier to test if scent makes you rush or if dry skin flushes easily.
4. **Extra acids.** Glycolic, salicylic, and peel blends in the same bottle can make the first week noisier than you wanted.
5. **Directions.** A few nights a week is a better first pass than nightly use plus another exfoliant.
6. **What comes after.** If you do not have a cream ready, the serum is only half the product.
7. **Current listing details.** Percents, scent, and sellers change, so read the page before buying.

The best first bottle is not the one with the biggest number on the label. It is the texture you can finish before someone yells that the permission slip is still on the counter.

## How to use a lactic acid serum without a harsh peel

Keep the order simple:

1. Cleanse with a gentle wash. Skip a grainy scrub.
2. Pat the face until it is almost dry.
3. Apply a thin layer of lactic serum. Do not sand dry patches.
4. Wait about a minute.
5. Apply moisturizer to seal the serum.
6. Use sunscreen in the morning as usual.
7. Start with two nights a week, then decide if a third night is worth it.

My weeknight rule is one acid, one cream, then lights out. If the product only feels good after a long sting, I save it for a slower night or admit the percent is not the one for this season.

If it leaves skin hot, tight, or shiny-but-dry, use it less often, drop the percent, or go back to cleanser and cream. Heat after an acid is a stop sign, not a reason to buy a stronger bottle the next day.

## FAQ

### Do I need a lactic acid serum if I already use a rich cream?

Not on every shelf. If a cream keeps flakes comfortable, you may not need a leave-on AHA. A lactic serum is worth comparing when makeup still catches on rough patches and when you can keep it to a few evenings.

### Can a lactic acid serum replace moisturizer?

No. A serum is not the seal. Dry skin that feels tight by morning is often missing cream, not a stronger percent.

### Why do flakes come back by lunch?

Indoor heat, a stripping cleanser, skipped moisturizer, and using too much acid can all look like "dryness that needs a peel." Try cream after the serum, then sunscreen in the morning, before adding a second exfoliant.

### Can I use lactic acid every night?

Follow the current label, then start with two or three evenings a week. Nightly lactic plus another acid is easy to overdo when mornings are already short.

### Can I use it in the morning?

You can if the finish settles under moisturizer and sunscreen. Many people keep lactic for night so an acid step does not fight the school-run clock. If mornings are already tight, do not add a sting test before drop-off.

### Is this the same as a chemical peel?

A leave-on 5% serum is not the same shopping lane as a timed peel pad. If the listing asks you to sit with a strong formula and rinse it off, you are in a different, fussier category than this article.
